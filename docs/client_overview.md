# Client Overview: Meeting Intelligence Workflow

## What it does

This workflow turns a meeting recording or transcript into a short leadership package.

For each meeting, it creates:

* A clear executive summary
* Three main objectives discussed in the meeting
* Three specific action items
* Next steps
* An editable slide deck
* A supporting summary document for review

The goal is to reduce the time spent writing meeting notes, following up on decisions, and creating leadership updates.

## How it helps

Today, teams often leave meetings with different understandings of what was decided and who is responsible for the next action.

This workflow helps by creating one consistent summary that is based on the meeting itself. It makes decisions, priorities, and action items easier to review and share.

For BrightLane, the first use case is leadership and operations meetings. It supports the goal of reducing manual reporting work, improving follow-up, and helping leaders focus on the most important issues.

## How the process works

1. A meeting recording or transcript is placed in the Meeting Intake folder.
2. The system prepares the meeting content.
3. It creates a draft summary and editable slide deck.
4. The files are placed in a Needs Review folder.
5. The reviewer receives a notification.
6. The reviewer approves, edits, rejects, or requests changes.
7. Approved files are moved to the Approved Outputs folder.

Nothing is sent to leadership automatically without human approval.

## What the reviewer receives

For each meeting, the reviewer receives:

* An editable PowerPoint or Google Slides deck
* A written meeting summary
* The meeting transcript
* A short list of risks or unclear points
* Evidence showing where key action items came from in the meeting

## Manual steps

The first version still requires a few simple actions:

* Ensure the meeting recording or transcript is available.
* Upload it to the Meeting Intake folder if it was not added automatically.
* Review the generated summary and deck.
* Correct wording, ownership, or priorities when needed.
* Approve the final version before sharing it.

These review steps are intentional. The system prepares the work, but people remain responsible for final decisions and communication.

## Feedback and changes

A reviewer can request content changes, for example:

* "Make this action item more specific."
* "Remove the deadline because it was not agreed."
* "Clarify who owns this task."
* "Use a more direct executive summary."

The system uses the original meeting transcript, the current summary, and the reviewer feedback to create a revised version.

Each revision is saved separately. Earlier versions are not overwritten.

## Output cadence

The workflow creates an output package each time a meeting file is added.

For a normal one-hour meeting, the expected cadence is:

* Meeting ends
* Recording or transcript becomes available
* Draft package is created
* Reviewer checks and approves it
* Final deck is available the same day

The exact timing depends on recording length, transcript availability, and whether revisions are needed.

## What this does not do yet

The first phase does not:

* Send messages to customers
* Make decisions without approval
* Replace operational reporting systems
* Automatically connect every business system
* Guarantee that every meeting statement is correct or complete

Later phases could connect operational data, customer support themes, inventory information, and weekly reporting into the same review process.
